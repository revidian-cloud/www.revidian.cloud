As the New Year's Eve ball dropped in New York City, I couldn't help but feel like I was dropping the ball with my current SaaS project, Melitix.
I identified issues that needed solving and honestly, I didn't feel like it.
After thinking it over in January, I've decided to pivot.
Here's why and what I'm building instead.

<!--more-->

## Obstacles

### The elephant in the room
I couldn't get people to try out Melitix.
That's a problem.
A multifaceted problem.
Before I list the reasons why I'm pivoting, I want to address a weakness of mine.
I have yet to get more than a couple of users for any SaaS project of mine.
Sure, some of my websites, CLI tools, Docker images, etc, have grown in usage but never one of my SaaS apps.
This is clearly a personal hurdle that I will need to overcome at some point.
I just no longer think that Melitix, at least right now, is the project to do that with.

### Reasons to pivot
It seems my Melitix thesis might be wrong.
Meetup.com started after the 9/11 terrorist attack.
The founder Scott Heiferman met with neighbors for the first time and realized how important community was.
That getting together was important.
After the world got through COVID-19 and social distancing, I expected a renaissance of gatherings and meet-ups.
I'm not seeing that play out.

I've reached out to many meet-up groups.
Some haven't had recent events, and others have done solely online meet-ups in the past few months or years.
When I ask why and if they plan to return to in-person events, I'm hit with complaints of commute, lack of events in smaller towns, and convenience, "why go when I can sit at home and attend?".
Organizers tell me they get a larger audience if they do the meet-up online.
This may be true, and there's nothing wrong with online events, but that's not a meet-up.
Maybe a webinar? Or something like that.
A meet-up is local in my mind.

While I was building Melitix to support online events (I don't live under a rock), I viewed them as the exception, not the default.
Features were being built around getting people physically together, finding venues, etc.
A predominately online space for meet-ups feels like an entirely different product and one I am less keen on building.

Another issue is the niches I was targeting.
The primary niche was tech.
It's a space I've worked in my entire career.
The years I've spent in developer relations (DevRel) really made me fond of meet-ups.
The vibe I've been getting across social media channels and in DevRel interviews I've been in tells me that tech folks seemed to be burnt out on meet-ups.
Honestly, it's all made me burnt out on this idea.


## Pivoting

### It won't disappear
I used the word "pause" in the title because that's what this is.
While I am moving on to another project, I am not purging Melitix and EventHunt, just pausing it.
I will still hold on to the names, the logos that I paid designers for, and the hope that this will one day be a viable project.
The code for Melitix, called EventHunt, was being built as an open-source project.
That [GitHub repository](https://github.com/melitix/eventhunt-webapp) will remain public and accessible.

### What's next
I have already started working on my new SaaS project called Revidian Cloud.
I need to work on my elevator pitch but at the moment, I describe it like this, "A management hub for all of your Internet assets \- domain names, services, TLS certificates, etc".
I see it helpful for solopreneurs and indie hackers with tons of domains and projects, as well as for large companies with several divisions and brands whose Internet assets might be a mess and prone to falling through the cracks.

Like Melitix, it's actually an old idea of mine that had a few false starts.
This time around, I will get an MVP up live for people (and myself) to use.
This new project is distinct from Melitix.
If you joined the Melitix mailing list, your subscription remains there and will not carry over to this project.
That's coming soon though.

In my next post I will cover starting the Revidian Cloud project and go over more of my ideas and intentions.
Stay tuned.
